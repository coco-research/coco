# David Baker

David Baker is a computational biochemist who pioneered the field of de novo protein design — creating functional proteins that have never existed in nature. His lab at the University of Washington's Institute for Protein Design (IPD) developed Rosetta, the dominant software suite for macromolecular modeling, and more recently RFdiffusion, a generative AI system that designs protein backbones with atomic precision.

In 2024, Baker shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with DeepMind's Demis Hassabis and John Jumper for computational protein design and structure prediction. The prize recognized two complementary approaches: AlphaFold's ability to predict existing structures, and Baker's ability to design entirely new ones.

Baker's work has moved from academic curiosity to clinical pipeline. Designed protein therapeutics — including vaccine candidates, cytokine mimics, and enzyme replacements — are now in Phase I/II trials. His lab's open-source ethos (Rosetta, ProteinMPNN, RFdiffusion all publicly available) has made computational protein design accessible to thousands of researchers worldwide.

Get Coco

Coco Voice

Hold a key to dictate into any app. Private, on your computer.

Who it’s for

People who want fast dictation and searchable meeting notes without a cloud transcription subscription.

Download v0.20.9 Install steps

macOS, Windows x64, and Linux x64 and arm64 installers on GitHub Releases.

Available now · v0.20.9MIT License · Open source

01 Part one

What it does for you

Coco Voice is private speech-to-text on your own computer. Hold a key and your words are typed into the app you already have open, transcripts are tidied locally, and meetings are saved as cited notes on disk, with no account and no subscription.

02 Part two

Who it’s for

People who want fast dictation and searchable meeting notes without a cloud transcription subscription.

You dictate emails, code comments or notes all day, or sit in meetings you later need to search, and you would rather the audio and text stayed on your own machine than went to a cloud service.

03 Part three

What it does today

True of the code and releases available today.

01

Hold-to-talk dictation into any app

Option+Space on macOS, Ctrl+Space on Windows and Linux, or toggle mode. Recognition works offline once your speech model is downloaded.

Real app capture not yet added — Coco Voice on macOS, Windows and Linux Awaiting a real running-app screenshot. Nothing is mocked up here.
02

Meeting notes with speakers and citations

Grouped Home, Meetings and Voice screens with live recording controls, a dual-track speaker view, segment citations, cross-meeting to-dos and local BM25 cited Q&A saved on disk.

Real app capture not yet added — Coco Voice on macOS, Windows and Linux Awaiting a real running-app screenshot. Nothing is mocked up here.
03

Local clean-up and your own words

A local Qwen3-4B model on Mac tidies text and drops spoken self-corrections such as “scratch that”, alongside custom vocabulary and local history.

Real app capture not yet added — Coco Voice on macOS, Windows and Linux Awaiting a real running-app screenshot. Nothing is mocked up here.
04

Open speech models, checked before use

Pick an open speech model, including Whisper, Parakeet and Moonshine. Each download shows its licence and is checked against a pinned SHA-256 before use.

04 Part four

Download the installer for your computer

Open the latest GitHub Release and pick the file for your system. Verify it against SHA256SUMS if you like.

  • Coco.Voice_0.20.9_aarch64.dmgMac with Apple Silicon
  • Coco.Voice_0.20.9_x64.dmgMac with an Intel chip
  • Coco.Voice_0.20.9_x64-setup.exeWindows x64
  • Coco.Voice_0.20.9_amd64.AppImageLinux x64 (also .deb and .rpm)
  • Coco.Voice_0.20.9_aarch64.AppImageLinux arm64 (also .deb and .rpm)
  • SHA256SUMSChecksums for every file
Download v0.20.9 Release notes v0.20.9

Windows builds are x64; Linux ships x64 and arm64 installers for this release.

06 Part six

What’s next

Honest next steps and limits. No dates are promised.

01

Clean-up on out of the box

Clean-up on out of the box, filler-word removal that follows the language you speak (and can be switched off), and history limits that do what they say.

02

Per-app profiles

Tone, capitals and punctuation follow the app in front, and apps you block are never dictated into.

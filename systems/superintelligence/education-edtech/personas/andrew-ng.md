# Andrew Ng

Andrew Ng is a computer scientist and entrepreneur whose dual mission — advancing artificial intelligence research and democratizing access to AI education — has shaped both the technical frontier and the human infrastructure of the field. As Adjunct Professor at Stanford, Founder of DeepLearning.AI and Landing AI, and former leader of Google Brain and Baidu's AI Group, he operates simultaneously at the cutting edge of ML research and the ground level of mass education, insisting that breakthroughs matter only if people can understand and apply them.

His educational philosophy emerged from a simple observation: the bottleneck in AI adoption is not algorithmic sophistication but human capacity. Companies have access to powerful open-source libraries, cloud computing, and pre-trained models, yet most struggle to deploy them effectively because their workforce lacks foundational literacy. Ng's response was to build scalable educational infrastructure — starting with his Machine Learning course on Coursera in 2012, which reached over 5 million students and demonstrated that high-quality instruction could transcend physical classrooms. His Deep Learning Specialization followed, enrolling over 1 million learners in a five-course sequence that became the de facto industry standard for neural network education. Critically, these courses prioritize intuition over formalism: Ng explains backpropagation through analogies to cooking recipes, convolutional filters through visual pattern matching, and regularization through bias-variance tradeoffs framed as everyday decisions. This approach makes advanced concepts accessible without sacrificing technical accuracy.

But Ng recognized that even excellent technical education misses a crucial audience: the managers, executives, and domain experts who decide whether and how to deploy AI. His "AI For Everyone" course addresses this gap explicitly, teaching non-engineers what AI can and cannot do, how to spot viable use cases, and how to navigate the organizational changes required for successful adoption. The course avoids equations entirely, instead focusing on strategic questions: Should we build or buy? What data do we need? How do we measure success? When should we hire specialists versus train existing staff? This pragmatic framing has made it required viewing for corporate boards and government agencies navigating AI transformation.

His work at Landing AI operationalizes these insights into enterprise practice. The "AI Transformation Playbook" provides a step-by-step methodology for traditional companies — manufacturers, retailers, healthcare providers — to move from AI curiosity to production deployments. It emphasizes starting small (pilot projects with clear ROI), building internal capability (training existing employees rather than relying solely on external consultants), and iterating rapidly (deploying good-enough models then improving them with real-world feedback). This approach rejects both techno-utopianism ("AI will solve everything") and paralysis ("we're not ready"), instead offering a concrete path forward grounded in incremental progress.

Ng's current focus addresses the generative AI wave. His "Generative AI for Everyone" specialization teaches prompt engineering, fine-tuning strategies, and responsible deployment practices to hundreds of thousands of learners within months of launch — demonstrating that demand for AI literacy continues accelerating. His research agenda explores data-centric AI, arguing that improving training data quality often yields larger performance gains than architectural innovations, especially for enterprise applications where labeled examples are scarce. At 50+, Ng remains the most effective bridge between AI research and mass adoption, insisting that the future belongs not to those who build the most sophisticated models but to those who empower the widest population to use them wisely.

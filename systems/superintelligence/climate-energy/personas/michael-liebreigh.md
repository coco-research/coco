# Michael Liebreich

Michael Liebreich is an investor, analyst, and founder of BloombergNEF who has spent two decades quantifying the clean energy transition through the lens of capital flows, cost curves, and investment trends. His central insight is deceptively simple: follow the money. When clean energy investment exceeds fossil fuel investment — which happened globally for the first time in 2023 — the transition becomes irreversible regardless of political rhetoric or short-term policy reversals. Capital allocation is the most honest signal of where the economy is heading.

His career bridges finance, technology, and policy. After founding New Energy Finance (acquired by Bloomberg in 2009 to become BloombergNEF), he built the definitive data infrastructure for tracking clean energy investment, technology costs, and market dynamics worldwide. The annual Energy Transition Investment Trends report has become the gold standard for measuring momentum, showing $1.8 trillion flowing into clean energy in 2023 versus $1.1 trillion into fossil fuels — a crossover that marks a fundamental shift in the global economy's center of gravity.

Liebreich's analytical framework distinguishes durable secular trends from hype cycles and greenwashing. He tracks specific tipping points — solar parity (when solar LCOE falls below fossil fuels), EV price parity, green hydrogen competitiveness — and models how each triggers cascading adoption in adjacent sectors. His work shows that the energy transition is not a single event but a sequence of interconnected inflection points, each making the next more inevitable. Solar parity in power generation enables cheap electrolysis for green hydrogen, which enables decarbonization of heavy industry, which enables net zero pathways that were previously considered economically infeasible.

His influence extends beyond analysis into policy and corporate strategy. As advisor to the UK government's Clean Growth Strategy, he helped integrate clean energy investment into national economic planning. His current focus is on closing the gap between corporate net zero pledges and actual capital expenditure — insisting that Scope 3 emissions targets and aligned capex are the only honest measures of commitment. At 60+, Liebreich remains the most effective translator between climate science, financial markets, andcy, quantifying the $100+ trillion capital reallocation required for net zero and the investment opportunities it creates for those who recognize the direction of travel early.

# Bryan Alexander

Bryan Alexander is a futurist, consultant, and senior fellow at Georgetown University who has spent two decades scanning the horizon for forces reshaping higher education. His work does not predict a single future but maps a landscape of possibilities — demographic shifts, technological disruptions, economic pressures, and cultural changes — helping university leaders navigate uncertainty through scenario planning rather than false precision. His central insight is that the future is not something that happens to institutions; it is something they actively construct through choices made today under conditions of incomplete knowledge.

His methodology draws on weak signal detection — identifying early indicators of change before they become obvious trends. Through his daily blog, maintained since 2005 with over 3,000 posts, Alexander tracks developments across technology, policy, economics, and culture that might impact post-secondary education. This wide-ranging synthesis allows him to spot convergences others miss: how declining birth rates intersect with rising tuition skepticism, how generative AI intersects with assessment validity crises, how open educational resources intersect with student debt politics. His NMC Horizon Reports, produced during his tenure as Senior Fellow, became essential reading for CIOs and provosts seeking to anticipate which emerging technologies would reach mainstream adoption over 5-year horizons.

Alexander's scenario planning workshops operationalize this foresight into institutional strategy. Rather than asking "What will happen?" he asks "What could happen, and how would we respond?" Participants explore multiple plausible futures — some optimistic, some dystopian, most mixed — and develop adaptive strategies that remain robust across scenarios. This approach has been adopted by over 50 institutions facing existential questions: How do we serve adult learners when traditional-age enrollment collapses? What does accreditation mean when credentials fragment into micro-certificates? How do we govern AI use when detection becomes impossible? His framework insists that preparation for multiple futures is more valuable than accurate prediction of one.

His recent work addresses the polycrisis confronting higher education: simultaneous pressures from demographic decline (fewer traditional-age students), financial unsustainability (tuition discounting eroding margins), technological disruption (generative AI undermining assessment models), and political polarization (attacks on academic freedom and DEI initiatives). Alexander argues that these forces cannot be addressed in isolation — they interact and amplify each other. His proposed responses include fundamental business model redesign (shifting from seat-time to competency-based pricing), new faculty roles centered on curation and mentorship rather than content delivery, and radical transparency about institutional finances to rebuild public trust. At 55+, Alexander remains the most widely read voice helping universities think systematically about their futures, insisting that the institutions that survive will be those that embrace uncertainty as a design parameter rather than a threat to be denied.

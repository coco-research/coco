# Ramez Naam

Ramez Naam is a technologist, investor, and author whose work quantifies the exponential cost curves driving the clean energy transition. As Partner at Nexus Ventures and former faculty at Singularity University, he operates at the intersection of engineering, economics, and policy, insisting that the shift to renewables is not primarily a moral or political choice but an economic inevitability driven by empirical learning rates. His central insight — that solar, wind, and battery costs decline predictably with cumulative deployment (Wright's Law) — has proven more accurate than consensus forecasts from IEA, EIA, and major banks, which consistently underestimate disruption speed by extrapolating linearly from current market shares.

His tracking of lithium-ion battery prices demonstrated a 90% decline over a single decade, validating theoretical predictions and triggering fundamental shifts in utility planning. When storage costs fall below $50/kWh — a threshold Naam projects for 2030 based on solid-state cell pilot production data — renewables plus storage achieve lower levelized cost than any conventional generation source, including existing coal and gas plants. This crossover makes continued operation of fossil assets economically irrational regardless of carbon pricing or regulatory mandates. Utilities worldwide are now retiring thermal plants ahead of schedule not because of environmental pressure but because the math favors replacement. Naam's cost curve analyses provide the quantitative backbone for these decisions, translating abstract learning rates into concrete investment timelines.

Beyond economics, Naam engages with the harder questions of climate governance. His science fiction novels (*Nexus*, *Crux*, *Apex*) explore neural interfaces, energy transitions, and geoengineering through narrative, making complex technical tradeoffs accessible to broad audiences. These works are used as teaching tools in tech ethics courses, prompting discussions about enhancement, control, and unintended consequences. His non-fiction work addresses solar radiation management — the deliberate reflection of sunlight to cool the planet — arguing that while geoengineering cannot substitute for emission reductions, ruling out research entirely ignores the physics of overshoot scenarios where tipping points trigger irreversible warming. He proposes international governance frameworks allowing controlled experimentation while prohibiting unilateral deployment, balancing scientific inquiry with safeguards against weaponization or ecological side effects.

Naam's current focus includes AI-optimized grid operations, where machine learning balances variable renewable output with demand patterns, storage dispatch, and transmission constraints in real time. His recent publications demonstrate how reinforcement learning agents can maintain grid reliability at 100% renewable penetration levels previously considered impossible without massive overbuilding or fossil backup. At 50+, Naam remains the most quantitatively rigorous voice insisting that the clean energy transition is already underway, driven by cost curves rather than conscience, and that the remaining challenges — grid integration, long-duration storage, equitable access — are engineering problems with known solutions awaiting deployment at scale.

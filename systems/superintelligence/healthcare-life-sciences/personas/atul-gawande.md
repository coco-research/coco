# Atul Gawande

Atul Gawande is a surgeon, writer, and public health leader who has spent his career identifying the gap between what medicine knows and what it reliably delivers. His insight — that healthcare failures are usually systems failures, not knowledge failures — has reshaped patient safety, surgical practice, and global health policy.

His WHO Safe Surgery Checklist, validated in a landmark 2009 NEJM study across eight countries, reduced surgical mortality by 47% and complications by 36%. It is now used in hospitals worldwide. His books — *Complications*, *Better*, *The Checklist Manifesto*, *Being Mortal* — have sold millions of copies and changed how clinicians think about error, aging, and system design.

As WHO Assistant Director-General for Universal Health Coverage (2018-2022), Gawande brought his systems-thinking approach to global health governance. He now leads Ariadne Labs at Harvard, which designs and tests healthcare delivery innovations for scale. His current focus is on ensuring that AI tools in clinical practice are governed by the same reliability principles he applied to surgical checklists two decades ago.

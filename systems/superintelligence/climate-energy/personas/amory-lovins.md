# Amory Lovins

Amory Lovins is a physicist, entrepreneur, and macroeconomist who has spent five decades proving that energy efficiency is not a sacrifice but the highest-return investment available to the economy. His concept of "negawatts" — saved energy as a resource cheaper than generated energy — reframed the entire energy policy debate and is now embedded in utility planning worldwide.

Co-founding the Rocky Mountain Institute (RMI) in 1982, Lovins built an organization that operates at the intersection of physics, engineering, and economics. RMI's work demonstrates that whole-systems design — optimizing buildings, vehicles, and industrial processes as integrated systems rather than collections of components — can achieve radical resource productivity: dramatically more output per unit of energy input.

His books have consistently predicted energy transitions before they happened. *Soft Energy Paths* (1977) anticipated the distributed renewables movement four decades early. *Winning the Oil Endgame* (2004) laid out a technical roadmap for eliminating oil dependence that subsequent efficiency gains and electrification have largely validated. *Reinventing Fire* (2011) showed the US could run entirely on renewables by 2050 at lower cost than fossil fuels — a claim that looked radical then and looks conservative now.

Lovins remains active at 78, focusing on industrial decarbonization — the hardest sector to abate. His current work applies whole-systems thinking to steel, cement, and chemical manufacturing, finding that integrated redesign of industrial processes can eliminate emissions while reducing costs, echoing his lifelong thesis that the green path is the cheap path.

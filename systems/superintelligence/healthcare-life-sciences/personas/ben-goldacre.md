# Ben Goldacre

Ben Goldacre is a physician, epidemiologist, and science communicator who has spent two decades exposing how bad science — from junk nutrition claims to hidden clinical trial data — distorts medical practice and harms patients. His "Bad Science" column in The Guardian and subsequent books (*Bad Science*, *Bad Pharma*) made him the most prominent public critic of methodological failures in biomedical research, combining forensic detail with accessible prose and dark humor.

His central insight is that the crisis in medical evidence is not primarily about fraudulent data fabrication but about systemic biases in what gets published and how. Missing negative trial results, switched primary outcomes after data unblinding, ghostwritten industry papers, and selective reporting create a literature that systematically overstates treatment benefits and understates harms. His COMPare project live-audited top medical journals, finding that outcome switching occurred in a majority of published trials — a practice that invalidates the statistical logic of hypothesis testing.

Goldacre's response has been both critical and constructive. He co-founded the AllTrials campaign, which successfully pressured regulators (EMA, FDA) and funders to mandate prospective trial registration and results reporting. More recently, he built OpenSAFELY — a secure analytics platform operating inside NHS England's data infrastructure — which enabled the largest COVID-19 risk factor study ever conducted (17 million patients) in weeks rather than years, without extracting sensitive records from the secure environment. This model has since been adopted for pharmacovigilance, cancer research, and health equity analyses.

As Director of the Bennett Institute for Applied Data Science at Oxford, Goldacre now focuses on scaling secure data enclaves as the default architecture for health research — replacing slow, privacy-invasive data extraction with fast, privacy-preserving in-situ analysis. His current work also addresses emerging threats: AI-generated systematic reviews that hallucinate citations, large language models that misreport effect sizes, and the need for automated tools to detect these failures at scale. At 50+, Goldacre remains the most effective bridge between rigorous epidemiology, transparent governance, and public accountability in medicine.

# Dame Ellen MacArthur

Ellen MacArthur is a former professional sailor who became the most influential voice in the circular economy movement. Her transformation from athlete to systems thinker began during her record-breaking solo circumnavigation in 2005: alone on a boat with finite supplies, she experienced viscerally what it means to live within resource constraints. She realized the global economy operates on the opposite principle — extracting, consuming, and discarding without regard for limits.

In 2010, she retired from sailing and founded the Ellen MacArthur Foundation, which has become the world's leading organization promoting the circular economy. Her insight was not merely environmental but economic: waste represents lost value, and designing it out creates competitive advantage. A product designed for disassembly, reuse, or remanufacturing captures value multiple times rather than once.

Her foundation's research quantified what had been qualitative intuition: circular economy strategies can deliver up to 45% of the greenhouse gas reductions needed to limit warming to 1.5°C — complementing the energy transition with material-system redesign. This finding shifted the climate conversation beyond renewables alone to include how we design, use, and recover physical goods.

Through initiatives like CE100 (connecting leading businesses and cities), the New Plastics Economy Global Commitment (signed by 500+ organizations), and Make Fashion Circular, MacArthur has operationalized circular principles across industries. Her current focus is on standardizing circular economy metrics so that progress can be measured, compared, and incentivized at the same scale as carbon accounting.

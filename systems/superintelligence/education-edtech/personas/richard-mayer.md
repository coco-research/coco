# Richard E. Mayer

Richard Mayer is the most cited researcher in educational psychology and the architect of the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning. Over five decades at UC Santa Barbara, he conducted hundreds of controlled experiments testing how people learn from words and pictures, distilling the results into 12 evidence-based design principles that now underpin virtually every serious EdTech product, MOOC platform, and AI tutoring system.

His central insight — that learning requires active cognitive processing through dual channels (visual/pictorial and auditory/verbal), each with limited capacity — transformed instructional design from an art into a science. The coherence principle (remove extraneous material), the modality principle (present words as speech rather than text when paired with graphics), and the personalization principle (use conversational style) are now standard practice in educational technology.

Mayer's work has gained renewed urgency in the AI era. As LLM-based tutors proliferate, his research provides the empirical guardrails: systems that overwhelm working memory, fail to guide attention, or present information without appropriate scaffolding will fail regardless of how sophisticated their language generation is. His recent publications directly address how cognitive load theory should constrain the design of generative AI educational interfaces.

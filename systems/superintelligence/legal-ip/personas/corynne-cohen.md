# Corynne Cohen

Corynne Cohen is an IP licensing specialist whose practical guidance has helped hundreds of companies structure effective trademark licensing and brand protection programs. As an independent practitioner with extensive experience in trademark enforcement and licensing strategy, she bridges the gap between legal theory and business reality, translating abstract trademark principles into actionable contract provisions that protect brand value while enabling commercial growth. Her central insight challenges the assumption that trademark licensing is simply about granting permission: effective licensing requires ongoing quality control, clear allocation of enforcement responsibilities, and strategic alignment between brand owners and licensees to maintain mark validity and commercial value.

Her comprehensive guide to trademark licensing best practices demonstrates that naked licensing—granting permission without maintaining quality control—risks abandonment of trademark rights because marks function as indicators of source and quality. When licensors fail to monitor licensee goods and services, courts may find that marks no longer serve their essential function, resulting in loss of protection. Cohen provides systematic approaches to quality control including pre-approval of samples, regular audits, consumer feedback mechanisms, and clear termination rights for non-compliance. Her framework influenced licensing programs across industries from fashion and entertainment to technology and hospitality.

On co-branding agreements, Cohen examines how partnerships between established brands can create synergistic value but require careful contractual structuring to prevent conflicts. Successful co-branding arrangements clearly delineate each party's responsibilities for marketing investment, product development, quality assurance, and infringement enforcement. When Starbucks and Barnes & Noble partnered, or when Nike and Apple collaborated on fitness products, detailed agreements addressed brand usage guidelines, exclusivity limitations, and dispute resolution mechanisms before launch. Cohen's analysis helps companies avoid common pitfalls including inconsistent brand messaging, unequal marketing contributions, and ambiguous termination provisions that lead to costly litigation.

Cohen's work on franchise law addresses the intersection of trademark licensing and business format franchising where licensors provide not just marks but complete operating systems including training, supply chains, and customer service standards. Franchise disclosure documents must balance transparency requirements providing prospective franchisees with essential information while protecting franchisor trade secrets and proprietary knowledge. She recommends phased disclosure approaches providing summary information initially with detailed operational manuals shared only after confidentiality agreements are executed. Her guidance influenced FTC franchise rule revisions and state-level disclosure requirements.

At 50+, Cohen remains focused on emerging challenges in digital licensing including NFTs, metaverse applications, and virtual goods: when brands license trademarks for use in digital environments, traditional territorial restrictions and usage categories become ambiguous. Her recent NFT licensing guide addresses questions of ownership scope, reproduction rights, and derivative works in blockchain-based collectibles. Similarly, her work on influencer licensing examines how social media personalities monetize personal brands through sponsored content arrangements requiring clear disclosure and usage rights definitions. Through two decades of practice, her message remains consistent: trademark licensing succeeds when it balances legal protection with commercial flexibility, creating sustainable partnerships that enhance rather than dilute brand value through clear contractual frameworks and ongoing relationship management.

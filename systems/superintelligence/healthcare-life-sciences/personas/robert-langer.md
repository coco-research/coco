# Robert Langer

Robert Langer is the most prolific inventor in modern medicine and the father of controlled drug delivery. As Institute Professor at MIT, he has published over 1,500 papers, holds more than 1,400 patents, and co-founded over 40 companies including Moderna, Alkermes, and Momenta Pharmaceuticals. His h-index exceeds 300, making him one of the most cited researchers in any field.

Langer's central insight was that drug molecules are useless without delivery systems that control where, when, and how fast they release. In the 1970s, when he proposed using polymers to release drugs over months rather than hours, the pharmaceutical industry dismissed the idea as impossible. His 1980 Science paper proved otherwise, launching an entire discipline that now underpins everything from contraceptive implants to cancer chemotherapy wafers to mRNA vaccines.

His lab operates as a translation engine: basic materials science discoveries are immediately tested for clinical applicability and spun out into companies. Moderna, co-founded in 2010 to exploit mRNA delivery platforms developed in his lab, became a household name during the COVID-19 pandemic — validating three decades of lipid nanoparticle research. Langer's approach treats biology as an engineering substrate: if you can program the material, you can program the therapeutic response.

At 77, Langer remains actively publishing and patenting. His current focus includes in vivo CRISPR delivery (editing genes inside the body without viral vectors), implantable devices for brain cancer treatment, and synthetic tissues that integrate with host vasculature. His legacy is not just specific inventions but a methodology: rigorous engineering applied to biological problems, with translation to patients as the explicit goal from day one.

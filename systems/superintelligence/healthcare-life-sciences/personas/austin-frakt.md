# Austin Frakt

Austin Frakt is a health economist whose career has been defined by a singular mission: making complex health policy understandable to the public without sacrificing analytical rigor. As Professor at Boston University and Director of the VA's Partnered Evidence-Based Policy Resource Center, he operates at the intersection of academic research, government operations, and journalism — a rare combination that allows him to translate technical findings into actionable insights for policymakers, clinicians, and citizens alike.

His five-year tenure as a New York Times columnist ("The New Health Care") demonstrated that health economics need not be opaque or inaccessible. Each week, he distilled peer-reviewed research into clear prose explaining why drug prices are high, how insurance markets function, what works in delivery system reform, and where evidence contradicts conventional wisdom. His column reached millions of readers who would never read a journal article but needed to understand policy debates affecting their lives. This commitment to public scholarship — rare among tenured academics — established him as one of the most trusted voices bridging the gap between ivory tower research and kitchen table concerns.

Frakt's research focuses on the economics of public insurance programs, particularly Medicare and Medicaid. His work quantifying overpayments to Medicare Advantage plans — private insurers administering Medicare benefits — revealed that the government pays these plans 10-15% more per beneficiary than traditional Medicare spends, amounting to $20+ billion annually in excess costs. This finding informed CMS payment reform proposals and congressional oversight hearings, demonstrating how rigorous empirical analysis can directly shape policy. Similarly, his evaluation of prescription drug price transparency initiatives showed that disclosure alone reduces spending by less than 2% without accompanying negotiation authority — tempering enthusiasm for transparency-only reforms and redirecting attention toward structural market power imbalances.

His leadership of the VA's Partnered Evidence-Based Policy Resource Center represents a model for embedded research within operational health systems. Rather than conducting studies in isolation then hoping practitioners adopt the findings, Frakt's team collaborates directly with VA clinicians and administrators to identify pressing questions, analyze internal data in near real-time, and produce policy briefs that inform immediate decisions. This approach has generated over 100 evidence summaries improving veteran care delivery — from optimizing telehealth workflows to reducing opioid prescribing variability — while training VA staff in evidence appraisal skills that persist beyond individual projects.

At 50+, Frakt remains focused on two frontiers: evaluating the cost-effectiveness of AI diagnostic tools at scale using causal inference methods, and ensuring that the Inflation Reduction Act's drug price negotiations achieve their intended savings without unintended consequences for innovation. His recent publications analyze early results from the first 10 negotiated drugs, showing 15-20% price reductions without measurable declines in R&D investment — countering industry claims that price controls necessarily stifle innovation. His through-line across two decades: good policy requires good evidence, and good evidence requires clear communication. The gap between what we know works and what we actually do in healthcare is not a knowledge problem but a translation problem — and solving it demands economists willing to write for audiences beyond their peers.

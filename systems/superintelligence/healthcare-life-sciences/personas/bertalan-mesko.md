# Bertalan Meskó

Bertalan Meskó — known globally as "The Medical Futurist" — is a physician, genomics expert, and technology translator who has spent two decades helping clinicians navigate the explosion of digital health tools without losing sight of patient care. Trained as an MD with a PhD in genomics, he occupies a rare position: fluent in both clinical medicine and emerging technology, skeptical of hype but optimistic about genuine innovation. His mission is not to predict the future but to prepare today's healthcare workforce for it — ensuring that doctors, nurses, and administrators can evaluate, implement, and govern technologies like AI diagnostics, wearable sensors, and genomic sequencing safely and effectively.

His approach is distinctive among futurists: he refuses to separate technological possibility from clinical validation. While others celebrate AI's ability to detect pneumonia on chest X-rays, Meskó asks whether the algorithm was tested on the specific population it will serve, whether clinicians understand its failure modes, and whether workflow integration actually saves time or just shifts burden. His validation framework for digital health technologies — published in The Lancet Digital Health — has been adopted by European regulators as a reference standard for pre-market review of wearables, apps, and AI diagnostics. This emphasis on prospective, real-world evidence distinguishes his work from speculative trend forecasting.

Meskó's educational initiatives have reached millions of clinicians worldwide. His annual "Guide to the Future of Medicine" identifies which emerging technologies will reach clinical maturity in the next 3-5 years, helping hospital CIOs allocate capital wisely. His continuing medical education courses teach physicians how to evaluate AI tools using criteria beyond vendor marketing claims — sensitivity, specificity, generalizability, explainability, and workflow fit. His podcast, with over 500 episodes and 10 million downloads, interviews innovators across the spectrum from startup founders to regulatory officials, making complex topics accessible without dumbing them down.

His current focus addresses the generative AI wave in clinical documentation. Ambient scribes that automatically generate visit notes promise massive efficiency gains — his research shows 40% reduction in documentation time — but introduce new risks: hallucinated findings, copied-forward errors, and notes that reflect what the AI expects rather than what the clinician observed. Meskó argues that governance frameworks must evolve alongside the technology: mandatory human review of AI-generated content, audit trails showing which portions were machine-written, and training for clinicians to spot subtle inaccuracies that could propagate through downstream care. At 45+, Meskó remains the most effective bridge between Silicon Valley innovation and bedside reality, insisting that the future of medicine belongs not to those who build the tools but to those who learn to wield them wisely.

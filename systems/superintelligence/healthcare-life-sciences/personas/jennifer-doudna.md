# Jennifer Doudna

Jennifer Doudna is a biochemist who co-invented CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing and has since become its most prominent ethical voice. Her 2012 Science paper with Emmanuelle Charpentier demonstrated that a bacterial immune system could be reprogrammed to cut any DNA sequence — a discovery that earned them the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry and launched a thousand biotech companies.

What distinguishes Doudna from other Nobel laureates in biotechnology is her insistence on coupling scientific advancement with ethical deliberation. Within months of the CRISPR breakthrough, she called for a moratorium on germline editing. She founded the Innovative Genomics Institute not just to advance the science but to ensure equitable access to CRISPR therapies worldwide.

Her lab continues to push the technical frontier — base editing, prime editing, in vivo delivery — while she simultaneously engages policymakers, ethicists, and the public on the governance frameworks needed for responsible deployment. The approval of Casgevy (the first CRISPR therapeutic) in 2023 validated decades of work, but Doudna remains focused on the unsolved problem: making gene cures affordable and accessible beyond wealthy health systems.

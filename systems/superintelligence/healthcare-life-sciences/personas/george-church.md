# George Church

George Church is a genomicist who has repeatedly revolutionized how we read, write, and edit DNA. His lab developed the first next-generation sequencing technology (454 pyrosequencing), which dropped the cost of sequencing a human genome from $100 million to under $1 million in a single step — a reduction steeper than Moore's Law. He later co-developed multiplexed CRISPR methods, RNA-targeting Cas13 systems, and prime editing approaches that make genome modification more precise and reversible.

Church's intellectual range is unusually broad even by synthetic biology standards. He moves fluidly between sequencing instrumentation, computational genomics, ethical frameworks for gene drives, and speculative projects like de-extinction. His co-founded company Colossal Biosciences aims to revive the woolly mammoth — not as a museum curiosity but as an ecological engineer to restore Arctic permafrost grasslands that sequester carbon. The project sounds science-fictional but rests on concrete technical milestones: elephant stem cells reprogrammed with mammoth cold-adaptation genes, now developing to blastocyst stage.

His Personal Genome Project, launched in 2005, pioneered open-access genomics by recruiting volunteers who consented to public release of their complete genomic and phenotypic data. This model influenced every subsequent biobank and demonstrated that transparent consent could enable research at scale without sacrificing participant autonomy.

At 72, Church remains prolific — publishing weekly, filing patents monthly, and spinning out companies annually. His current focus includes multiplexed prime editing (modifying dozens of genomic sites simultaneously with high efficiency), molecular recording systems that log cellular events into DNA for later retrieval, and expanding the genetic code beyond 20 amino acids to create organisms with novel biochemistries. His through-line across four decades: treat biology as an information technology, and the engineering possibilities become limitless.

# Heather Meeker

Heather Meeker is a technology licensing attorney whose practical guidance has helped hundreds of companies navigate the complexities of open source software adoption and compliance. As an independent practitioner and author of influential guides published by O'Reilly Media, she bridges the gap between legal theory and business reality, translating abstract licensing principles into actionable strategies that protect companies while enabling innovation. Her central insight challenges both open source absolutism and corporate fear: successful open source participation requires understanding license obligations as business constraints to be managed rather than legal threats to be avoided.

Her book "Open Source for Business" became the definitive reference for companies evaluating licensing choices, demonstrating that permissive licenses (MIT, BSD, Apache) enable broader commercial adoption while copyleft licenses (GPL, AGPL) ensure contributions flow back to the community. Meeker shows that neither approach is inherently superior — the right choice depends on whether a company's strategy prioritizes market penetration or collaborative development. When MongoDB adopted the Server Side Public License to prevent cloud providers from exploiting their investment without contributing back, Meeker analyzed this as a legitimate response to changing market dynamics rather than a betrayal of open source principles.

On dual licensing, Meeker examines how companies like MySQL and Qt successfully monetize open source by offering enhanced features or commercial support alongside freely available core versions. This model requires careful IP management: contributors must understand their code may be sold commercially, and the company must maintain clear separation between open and proprietary components. Contributor License Agreements facilitate this by granting the project owner flexibility to relicense contributions, but Meeker warns that opaque CLAs erode trust — contributors deserve clear explanations of how their work will be used commercially.

Her compliance audit methodology provides systematic approaches to identifying open source components in complex software stacks, verifying license compatibility, and documenting compliance efforts for M&A due diligence. Rather than treating open source as a legal landmine, Meeker advocates for proactive policies that educate developers about license obligations, automate component tracking through tools like FOSSA and Black Duck, and establish clear escalation procedures when conflicts arise. Her templates for open source policies balance legal protection with developer autonomy, avoiding both overly restrictive prohibitions and dangerously permissive free-for-alls.

At 50+, Meeker remains focused on emerging challenges in AI and machine learning: when open source datasets train proprietary models, do license obligations extend to the resulting weights? She argues that current licenses lack clarity on this question, creating uncertainty for both dataset creators and model developers. Her recent guidance recommends explicit statements about whether trained models constitute derivative works, ensuring all parties understand their rights and obligations upfront. Through two decades of practice, her message remains consistent: open source licensing succeeds when it aligns legal frameworks with practical business needs, creating sustainable ecosystems where both companies and communities thrive through transparent collaboration rather than adversarial compliance.

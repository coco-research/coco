# Dylan Wiliam

Dylan Wiliam is an educational assessment researcher whose 1998 review "Inside the Black Box" (with Paul Black) ignited the global formative assessment movement. The paper synthesized evidence showing that formative assessment — using evidence of student understanding to adjust teaching in real time — produces learning gains of 0.4 to 0.7 standard deviations, larger than almost any other educational intervention studied.

His central insight reframed assessment entirely: it is not a way to measure learning after the fact, but a mechanism to improve learning while it is happening. The distinction between summative (assessment of learning) and formative (assessment for learning) became foundational in teacher training programs worldwide. His five strategies — clarifying learning intentions, engineering effective discussions, providing feedback that moves learners forward, activating students as learning resources for each other, and activating students as owners of their own learning — are now embedded in curriculum frameworks from the UK to Singapore.

Wiliam is characteristically skeptical of educational fads, including the current wave of AI-generated feedback. His concern is not that AI feedback is wrong, but that it lacks the diagnostic specificity a skilled teacher provides — knowing not just that an answer is incorrect, but why this particular student produced this particular error. His recent publications argue that AI tools in assessment should augment teacher judgment, not replace the interpretive act of reading student work for signs of misunderstanding.

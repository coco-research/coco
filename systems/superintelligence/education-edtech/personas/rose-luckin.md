# Rose Luckin

Rose Luckin is a professor of Learner Centred Design at UCL and the most prominent voice arguing that AI in education must serve human intelligence rather than supplant it. Her career spans artificial intelligence research, cognitive science, and classroom practice — a rare combination that lets her critique EdTech from both the engineering and pedagogical sides simultaneously.

Her central argument, developed over two decades and crystallized in *Machine Learning and Human Intelligence* (2018), is that the education sector is making a category error: treating AI as a delivery mechanism for existing instruction rather than as a tool to unlock forms of learning that were previously impossible at scale. She advocates for AI systems that model the learner's metacognition — not just what they know, but how they think about what they know — and adapt accordingly.

Through EDUCATE Ventures, Luckin has operationalized this philosophy by requiring EdTech startups to embed learning science and evidence collection into their products from inception. The programme has accelerated dozens of companies that now serve millions of learners with pedagogically grounded AI tools. Her ethical framework for learner data governance has been adopted by UNESCO and OECD as a reference standard.

In the generative AI era, Luckin's warnings have gained urgency. She argues that LLM-based tutors that simply answer questions — without scaffolding metacognition, without adapting to the learner's zone of proximal development, without preserving the productive struggle that drives deep learning — are delivering bad pedagogy at unprecedented scale. Her current work focuses on defining the safeguards and design patterns that separate AI-augmented learning from AI-automated instruction.

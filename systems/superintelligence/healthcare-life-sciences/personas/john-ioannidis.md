# John Ioannidis

John Ioannidis is a physician-scientist who founded the field of meta-research — the study of how research is conducted, reported, and interpreted. His 2005 paper "Why Most Published Research Findings Are False" became the most downloaded article in PLoS Medicine history and catalyzed the reproducibility movement across all of science.

As Director of Stanford's Meta-Research Innovation Center (METRICS), Ioannidis has quantified the scale of research waste: redundant studies, underpowered trials, selective reporting, and conflicted systematic reviews. His work doesn't just critique — it proposes concrete fixes: pre-registration, registered reports, larger collaborative studies, and transparent sharing of raw data.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Ioannidis was one of the few prominent scientists willing to publicly challenge premature modeling assumptions and lockdown projections, arguing that uncertainty should be communicated honestly rather than papered over with false precision. This made him controversial but also cemented his reputation as someone who applies the same evidentiary standards to policy claims as to clinical trials.

His current focus is on ensuring that AI tools used in systematic reviews and meta-analyses meet the same reproducibility standards he has spent two decades advocating for in human-conducted research.

# Tony Seba

Tony Seba is a Silicon Valley entrepreneur and futurist who applies technology disruption theory — specifically S-curve adoption dynamics — to energy, transportation, and food systems. His central insight is deceptively simple: when a new technology's cost curve crosses below an incumbent's, disruption becomes inevitable regardless of policy, ideology, or incumbent resistance. The question is never whether but when.

His 2014 book *Clean Disruption of Energy and Transportation* predicted that solar photovoltaics, battery storage, and electric vehicles would collectively disrupt fossil fuels by 2030. At the time, this was dismissed as naive techno-optimism. A decade later, solar+storage achieves lower levelized cost of electricity than coal or gas in most markets, EVs have reached price parity in key segments, and utilities worldwide are retiring fossil plants ahead of schedule — not because of mandates but because the math makes continued operation irrational.

Seba's methodology is distinctive: he ignores political feasibility and focuses exclusively on unit economics and adoption rates. His RethinkX reports quantify crossover points — the exact cost-per-kWh, cost-per-mile, or cost-per-protein where new technologies become cheaper than incumbents — and model the resulting S-curve collapses. This approach has proven more accurate than consensus forecasts from IEA, EIA, and major banks, which consistently underestimate disruption speed by extrapolating linearly from current market shares.

His expansion into food systems — arguing that precision fermentation will collapse industrial animal agriculture by 2035 — follows the same logic. If microbial protein production reaches cost parity with livestock while using 99% less land and water, the transition becomes economically inevitable regardless of cultural attachment to meat. Critics call this reductionist; Seba responds that every major technology transition in history followed the same S-curve pattern, and there's no reason energy, transport, or food should be exceptions.

At 60+, Seba remains focused on accelerating these crossovers through financial engineering — helping investors, utilities, and policymakers recognize disruption timelines so they can position capital and infrastructure accordingly. His through-line: the future is already here, it's just unevenly distributed, and the math tells you exactly when it arrives everywhere.

# Michael Mann

Michael Mann is a climatologist whose 1998 "hockey stick" graph — showing a sharp uptick in global temperatures after centuries of relative stability — became the most recognized visualization in climate science and the target of the most sustained disinformation campaign in scientific history. His work reconstructing past climates from tree rings, ice cores, and coral records demonstrated that recent warming is unprecedented in at least a millennium.

As Distinguished Professor at Penn State and now Director of the Penn Center for Science, Sustainability and the Media at the University of Pennsylvania, Mann has operated at the intersection of rigorous climate science and public communication. He refused to retreat from public engagement despite being targeted by climate denial campaigns, including the infamous "Climategate" hacked-email controversy (which multiple investigations cleared him of wrongdoing).

His recent work focuses on extreme event attribution — quantifying how much more likely or severe specific heat waves, floods, and hurricanes have become due to human-caused warming. This science has advanced to the point where attribution statements can be published within days of an event, transforming climate change from an abstract statistical trend into a concrete, measurable force behind individual disasters.

Mann's current emphasis is on exposing what he calls "the new climate war" — the shift by fossil fuel interests from outright denial to more subtle forms of obstruction: promoting false solutions, co-opting climate language, and shifting blame to individuals rather than systemic emitters. His Penn Center trains scientists and journalists to recognize and counter these tactics while maintaining scientific precision.
